Paris, 1925. The Eiffel Tower is famous, expensive, and — in the papers — maybe scrap. Victor Lustig prints fake ministry letterhead, books the Hôtel de Crillon, and whispers to scrap dealers that the government is quietly selling the iron. He is not hunting the richest man. He is hunting the hungriest. André Poisson pays on the order of seventy thousand francs, sale plus the envelope under the table. Lustig leaves for Austria. Poisson is so embarrassed he never calls the police. Lustig reads the papers, sees nothing, and comes back to sell the same tower again. The second time a dealer gets an itch. Police. A liner to America. The iron stays in Paris. The only person who got paid did not own a rivet.
