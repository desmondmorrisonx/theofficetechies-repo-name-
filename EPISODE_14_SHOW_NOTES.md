### Description

The unfiltered ask from Episode 13 worked. The show has a real 21st listener — someone's grandmother, who now texts weekly to ask whether "the hallway man" is doing okay — and its first real donation: three dollars and fifty cents, attached to a blunt demand that Alex finally buy a chair instead of sitting on a pallet with a cushion.

What follows is Alex and Morgan celebrating one single new listener with the full pageantry of a company that just closed a funding round, and never once breaking the bit. Morgan reveals the sticky-note tally she kept of every time Alex said "hallway" in Episode 13. The final count was four. The donation note gets read on air, verbatim: *"buy a chair. sitting on a stack of pallets is not a load-bearing personality."*

Then comes the callback that makes the episode. Two weeks after roasting fifty-person agencies that need a meeting to schedule a meeting, the studio held a meeting to discuss whether to schedule the purchase of a chair. Attendance: two people and one very judgmental cat who walked across Alex's keyboard. The cat voted no. The cat likes the pallet.

Underneath the jokes the numbers are real and unrounded: twenty-one listeners, one $3.50 donation, which the studio is matching itself and putting straight into equipment. A big agency would call this "audience growth trending positive." We call it one grandmother and three dollars fifty, because only one of those is an actual number.
